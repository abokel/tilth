# How Crops Grow

Crops develop on **growing degree days**: each day adds its average temperature above the crop's base temperature. Wheat counts from 0 °C, maize from 10 °C. Warm weather speeds them, cold weather stops them.

Seeds need warm, moist soil to sprout. Warm-season seed sitting in cold, wet soil for weeks will **rot**.

The ten visible stages follow the **BBCH scale** used by agronomists: emergence, leaves, tillering, stem elongation, heading, flowering, grain fill, ripening, ripe.

Each day the crop grows as fast as its scarcest resource allows: water, a nutrient, pH or light, adjusted for temperature, compaction and disease. Right-click a crop to see what is limiting it.

**Frost** kills tender crops (tomato, beans, maize) just below 0 °C. Hardy crops such as rye and garlic survive deep frost, though all crops are most sensitive while flowering. **Heat** above about 32-38 °C at flowering sterilises grain.
