# Cultivars & Seed Saving

Every plant carries **genes**, two copies of each: four genes each for growth, yield, drought tolerance and frost tolerance, a **resistance** gene and a **semi-dwarfing** gene. Each seed gets one copy of every gene from each parent, at random, as Mendel found.

Seed you save at harvest inherits by how the crop sets seed. **Self-pollinating** crops (wheat, barley, oats, rice, peas, beans, lentils, tomatoes) fertilise themselves: each generation is more uniform, and a line soon **breeds true**.

**Cross-pollinating** crops (maize, rye, sunflowers, cabbages, onions, beets, carrots, clovers) take pollen from plants of the same crop within four blocks. A cross of two distinct lines shows **hybrid vigour**; a lone plant that can only pollinate itself loses vigour generation by generation. Seed saved from an F1 hybrid **segregates** and is not as good.

**Potatoes and garlic** grow from tubers and cloves: each is a copy of its parent.

**Resistance** is dominant: one copy keeps most infection of the diseases that have resistant varieties (the rusts, blights, mildews, cyst nematodes and more) from taking hold. **Semi-dwarfing** is recessive: only plants with both copies are short-strawed, and in wheat, barley, oats and rice they put more into grain.

A seed's tooltip shows its cultivar and generation (F1 hybrid, F3 segregating, true-breeding), its traits, and whether it carries resistance or is a semi-dwarf. **Pollinators** matter too: insect-pollinated crops set more seed with flowers and working bees nearby.
