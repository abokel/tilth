# Soil Texture

Soil is a mix of **sand** (gritty, drains fast, holds little), **silt** (smooth, holds water well) and **clay** (sticky, holds water and nutrients but drains slowly). The mix decides the texture class, one of the twelve USDA classes, plus **peat**.

> **Sand Topsoil**  
> **Sand and loamy sand** warm up early and are easy to work, but dry out fast, lose nitrogen to leaching and need frequent manure.

> **Loam Topsoil**  
> **Loams** (loam, silt loam, sandy loam) are the best all-round farm soils: good water, good drainage and good fertility.

> **Clay Topsoil**  
> **Clays** hold the most water and nutrients but stay wet and cold in spring, compact easily and smear if worked wet.

> **Peat**  
> **Peat** is half organic matter. It forms in cold, wet hollows, holds huge amounts of water and is naturally very acidic.

> **Peat Brick**  
> Dug peat breaks into four **Cut Peat**: a fuel that burns half as long as coal. Dry it in a furnace into **Peat Bricks**, which burn as long as charcoal.

Texture also sets how far water seeps sideways from a ditch or pond: about two blocks through sand and up to five or six through silts, clays and peat. Fine soils draw water by capillarity.
