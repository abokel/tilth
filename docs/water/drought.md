# Drought & Waterlogging

When roots cannot draw enough water, growth slows. Prolonged drought (about two weeks with almost no available water) kills a crop.

Stress at **flowering** costs the most yield: the same drought at tillering costs far less.

Waterlogged soil suffocates roots, and nitrogen is lost to the air (denitrification). Clay depressions and toeslopes are most at risk. Plant water-tolerant crops there, such as rice, clover and sorghum, or drain them.
